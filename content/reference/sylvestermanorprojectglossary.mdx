# Sylvester Manor Project Glossary

**Barbados:** Island in the eastern region of the Caribbean. During slavery, the island had a sugar dominated slave economy where enslaved people faced high mortality and brutal conditions.

**bill of sale:** A legal document that transferred the ownership of personal property from a buyer to a seller. In the context of slavery, it was used to transfer the ownership of an enslaved person from buyer to seller.

**creolization:** The process by which cultural, racial, or linguistic elements are blended to create a new cultural identity. 

**free people of color:** People of African or mixed African and European ancestry who were not legally enslaved in societies where slavery existed. The term is typically associated with French colonies (namely the Gens de couleur libres of Colonial Louisiana) where free people of color existed as a distinct social class. The term encompasses people who were born free and formerly enslaved people who obtained their freedom. 

**indentured servitude:** A system in which a person agreed to do work for another person for a set period of time, typically in exchange for transportation to a new country, food, housing, or the repayment of a debt. During the colonial period in America, indentured servants agreed to work for several years to pay for their passage from Europe to the colonies. In return, they received basic necessities, but typically had limited freedoms under their contracts. Once their contracts ended, they were free to leave and pursue their own lives. 

**Indigenous people:** The original inhabitants of a particular region and their descendants, who have maintained historical connections to the land, culture, traditions, and communities that existed before colonization. In the Americas, Indigenous peoples include Native American tribal nations in North America and Indigenous groups throughout Central and South America.

**Long Island:** Island in southeastern New York. Originally called “Sewanhacky” (Island of Shells) before European colonization by Algonquian speaking Indigenous people. 

**Manhansett people:** Indigenous people that settled on Shelter Island in 3500 BCE.

**manumission:** An enslaved person being freed by their enslaver through a will, a legal document, verbal agreement, or by buying their own freedom.

**Mulatto:** A term used to describe a person of mixed African and European ancestry. It was commonly used during the colonial period especially in societies where people were classified by race. Sometimes seen as a separate social class from “Negro.” These racial classifications influenced a person’s legal status, social standing, and rights. Today, the term is considered outdated and offensive depending on the context. 

**Negro:** A term used to describe people of African descent. Commonly used in the United States and other parts of the world from colonization up until the mid-60s. Though once used in everyday language, it is generally considered outdated and may be seen as offensive depending on the context.

**provisional plantation:** A plantation established specifically to supply food, livestock, and raw materials for sugar plantations in the Caribbean. 

**settler colonialism:** A form of colonization where people from another country settle permanently in a region and establish a new society, often taking land from the Indigenous people that already live there. 

**Shelter Island:** An island located between the North and South Forks of Long Island, New York. Originally called Manhansack-aha-quash-awamock, meaning “an island sheltered by islands” in Algonquian. The original inhabitants of the island are the Manhanset people who have lived on Shelter Island for thousands of years before Europeans arrived in the 17th century. 

**slavery:** A system in which one person is treated as the property of another and is forced to work without freedom. Enslaved people are denied basic rights and control over their own lives, and their labor is often taken through violence or threats. In the Americas, slavery was significant during the colonial period and the 19th century, when millions of Africans were transported during the transatlantic slave trade and forced to work on plantations, in households, and in other industries such as ironwork, carpentry, maritime work, and construction. 

**sugar during slavery:** Highly profitable cash crop that drove the expansion of slavery, particularly in the Caribbean. Sugar plantations relied heavily on the labor of enslaved Africans, who planted, harvested, and processed sugarcane under brutal conditions. The high demand for sugar in Europe and other parts of the world generated massive  wealth for plantation owners and contributed to the growth of the transatlantic slave trade. Some major producers of sugar during the colonial era include: Haiti, Cuba, Louisiana, Barbados, and Brazil. 

**West Indies:** A group of islands in the Caribbean sea. The term was coined by Christopher Columbus in 1492 after he believed he reached the eastern coast of Asia when he was actually in the Americas.

**will:** A legal document that states how a person wants their money, property, land, possessions, and other assets to be distributed after their death. It can also name the person responsible for carrying out their wishes. During slavery, wills were sometimes used to pass down land, money, and enslaved people to their children and other relatives. In some cases, instructions were included to free enslaved people.
